## Problem

What is broken or missing, and who feels it. Concrete beats abstract.

## Assumptions

Everything this design takes for granted. Mark each one as measured, reasoned, or unverified. An
unverified assumption is not a failure, but pretending it is measured is.

| Assumption | Basis | Risk if wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Design

What is being built. Use diagrams where a diagram is clearer than a paragraph, and prose where it is
not.

## Alternatives considered

At least two, each with why it was not chosen. "We did not think of anything else" is a sign the
design has not been stress-tested yet.

| Alternative | Trade-off | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Failure modes

What breaks, what the blast radius is, and how the system behaves while it is broken. Include the
cases where a component is slow rather than dead, which are usually worse.

## Performance implications

State the expected effect and say plainly whether it is measured or predicted. Unproven numbers are
labelled as such, every time.

## Complexity

Roughly how much work this is, and what it makes harder to change later.

## Security and tenancy

Who can reach what, and what a hostile tenant or compromised node can do with this change.

## Open questions

The ones the design does not settle. An RFC may be accepted with open questions if they are recorded
here and none of them are load-bearing.
